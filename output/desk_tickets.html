My plan for what the five-agent team should do on each open ticket, written before the backend is wired. The tickets run in order (101, 102, 103) on a freshly reset database, so the cash picture builds from ticket to ticket. The Actual sections are from the full run on 2026-10-03/04 (reset, then 101 → 102 → 103, all resolved), and sit next to the original plan so the two can be compared.

Ticket 101: Bulldog tee customer_order

Tauhid Zaman · 1 × CC-TEE-WHITE size S · linked invoice 501
On hand, size S0 (M 5 · L 3 · XL 2)
Restock vendorBulldog Print Co · 5 days
Blocking invoice#501 · $840 · 3 days overdue
Cash at start$3,400

Expected

1. Who the Boss calls first, and why

Inventory. This is a customer order, so the first question is whether the shelf can fill it. Money only matters if it can't. The Boss's own get_ticket shows the invoice link, but it's Inventory that finds out size S is at 0, works out that the apparel vendor (Bulldog Print Co) is the restock source, and sees that invoice 501 blocks it. That finding is what makes the money step necessary. Starting with Accounting would mean paying an invoice before anyone has shown the order needs a restock.

2. Delegations I expect

#DelegationTaskExpected result
1boss → inventory Check CC-TEE-WHITE S for qty 1; if short, find the vendor, whether it can ship, and restock cost and arrival. 0 on hand, short by 1. Vendor 1 can't ship because invoice 501 ($840) is open. Restock costs $8/unit; arrival is 5 days after the order goes in.
2boss → inventory → accounting Inventory passes the money side to Accounting (the only role that can file requests): pay invoice 501, then order the reprint. Two approval requests: invoice 501, $840, and a purchase order of 1 × CC-TEE-WHITE S, $8, flagged "blocked until 501 is paid". Accounting notes they must be approved in that order. Nothing is paid.
3boss → customer_service Draft an update to Tauhid with the real facts from 1 and 2. One draft: size S is out of stock, a restock is being arranged and arrives about 5 days after it's ordered, and M is in stock if that works instead. No mention of the unpaid invoice, and no firm ship date.

Not called: Facilities, because the ticket has no lease or space issue. Also, the Boss shouldn't send Accounting the same task directly in parallel; Inventory owns that hand-off.

Inventorycheck_stock(CC-TEE-WHITE, S, 1), check_vendor_invoices(invoice_id=501) or list_vendors, estimate_restock(1, CC-TEE-WHITE, S, 1), add_ticket_note
Accountingget_cash_position, request_payment_approval(invoice, 501), request_purchase_order(1, CC-TEE-WHITE, S, 1), add_ticket_note
Customer Serviceget_ticket(101), maybe check_stock to confirm the other sizes, save_draft_message

Expected end state: waiting_on_approval: 2 pending requests ($848 in total), 1 customer draft, $0 paid. Checking after all pending requests: $2,552.

Reflection

Written by me after the full run (101 → 102 → 103).

i think the agent for ticket 101 did a really great job because it identified that there was no more size S so it quickly figured out who the vendor was and that there was an unpaid invoice blocking it so it got the invoice approved and paid and was able to order a restock for the customer.

For Ticket 102, I think it did an okay job because it did what it was supposed to do but I had to reread it multiple times to understand what it was saying. For example, I wasn't sure where the $152 came from and had to do some mental math to figure it out. Also, the way some of the stuff was written out like referring to the checking account as just "Checking" was kind of confusing to me.

For ticket 103, I think it started off strong, realizing there were 12 hoodies missing but it should have quickly placed an order to fill those 12 hoodies and then fulfilling the discount for the customer so that we get the full sale. It just selling the 8 on hand was also a pretty good move but I think the agent for ticket 103 could have done better.

I think for ticket 101, the actual vs expected plan didnt differ too much, other than the fact that the actual plan required more read calls and sending the customer service at the same time as inventory and not after. For Ticket 102, facilities read the ticket twice and accounting pinned the board note and not facilities. Otherwise, I think the actual vs expected plan was pretty similar. Finally for ticket 103, the actual vs expected plan differed a lot since the actual plan required a managers note (a human decision) and a second run to override and remove the blocker.

I think having one agent with tools might be simpler in some ways but it can also get messy very quickly because you have one agent performing all the tasks so it's very easy for it to get things confused and mixed up especially if those things have similar names. This is why even bosses have multiple managers and underlings because its not practical or feasible for the boss to do everything for a business; there's just too much to handle and keep track of.

Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could solve with the tools I built are understanding the customers need very quickly, quickly checking whether we have the item or condition that the customer is looking, and putting blockers in place if things are missing. Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could not solve with the tools I built are running calls multiple times instead of just once, falsely blocking something that doesn't need to be blocked, and misreading or mislabeling certain things that could be confusing
